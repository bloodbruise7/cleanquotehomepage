# CleanQuote homepage update brief

Prepared September 25, 2026. Website: https://thecleanquote.com/

Status: local research and proposed update, ready for review. This document does not change or publish the website.

Primary conversion: **Start Free Trial**, confirmed by Brandon for this update.

## 1. Recommended direction

Show cleaning business owners the complete job lifecycle within the first two sections: a prospect requests a quote, approves it, books an available appointment, and becomes work the team can deliver and collect payment for. Give the pricing calculator a prominent supporting role, with its connection to actual crew hours as the deeper reason to choose CleanQuote.

The current page has useful product screenshots and unusually specific feature explanations. Its ordering makes CleanQuote look primarily like a calculator with other tools attached. The update should make the operating system obvious immediately and let visitors explore the details after they understand the benefit.

Recommended positioning:

> Cleaning business software that connects your website, quotes, recurring schedule, team, and payments, with estimates informed by the hours your crews actually work.

Lead with the visible customer experience. Demonstrate the operational depth. Explain the pricing feedback loop. Make it easy to start a trial.

Recommended initial audience: owners of residential cleaning companies, including owner-operators and businesses coordinating multiple cleaners. This follows the current product positioning. Commercial janitorial contracts, vacation-rental turnovers, and franchise operations should not become primary promises without separate product validation and an explicit audience decision.

Brandon previously explored an advertising angle showing the self-service booking flow. That saved idea supports this recommendation, but is context rather than a binding positioning decision.

### What this research establishes

All eight requested URLs were inspected. Text extraction was supplemented with live browser inspection, including the Housecall Pro cleaning page that the text browser could not retrieve. Desktop hero visuals were reviewed. CleanQuote's local website files and selected recent application files were also inspected.

These observations establish what the companies are presenting and the objections their pages address. They do **not** establish which elements caused conversions. No competitor analytics, experiment results, CleanQuote funnel data, or customer interviews were available. Conversion mechanisms and proposed improvements below are hypotheses to test. Vendor growth statistics and testimonials are vendor-presented evidence, not independently established causal results.

## 3. Where CleanQuote can stand apart

“Built for cleaning businesses,” scheduling, reminders, booking forms, payments, and an operator-founder story already appear in this competitive set. They are valuable, but cannot carry an exclusive claim.

| Positioning asset | What to demonstrate | How to describe it responsibly |
|---|---|---|
| Connected self-service acquisition | Website details → generated quote → customer approval → available appointment → scheduled visit. | A concrete reason to try the product. Online booking itself is common. Do not claim CleanQuote is the only platform that connects these steps. |
| Estimated hours connected to actual hours | A home estimate, the crew's recorded time, and the calibration/reporting view. | The most distinctive product story to investigate further. Say what the system does. Do not claim proven accuracy gains or exclusive technology from this page review. |
| Scheduling that accounts for customer preferences | Recurring visit, preferred cleaner/day/window, and an Organizer proposal before approval. | Show how a real weekly schedule is handled. Routing and preferred-cleaner support are not exclusive to CleanQuote. |
| Meaningful owner review points | Proposed schedule changes and invoice drafts, with the relevant review step visible. | Explain control at the specific action level. Avoid implying that every automatic action requires approval. |
| Broad functionality at transparent entry pricing | A concise scope summary beside the actual plan and Communications Hub costs. | Support value with visible capabilities. Do not claim lowest price, unlimited users, or every communication feature included in the base plan. |
| An operator's actual product experience | Brandon demonstrating a specific cleaning-business problem and the screen that addresses it. | Credibility through specificity. Founder affiliation must be disclosed in any Reliable Housekeeping case study. It is not independent customer testimony. |

The strongest combined story is the relationship between winning work, delivering recurring cleans, and learning from actual labor. The review does not establish an exclusive market position for that combination.

## 4. Proposed homepage, in reading order

### A. Header and hero

Navigation: **How it works · Features · Pricing · FAQ**. Keep Log In visually secondary. Keep Start Free Trial visible on small screens.

Proposed hero copy:

> **Cleaning business software for residential cleaning companies**
>
> # Turn website visitors into booked cleanings.
>
> Let customers request a quote, approve it, and choose an available time online. Manage recurring visits, your team, and payments in the same place.
>
> **Start Free Trial**
>
> See how a cleaning gets booked
>
> 14 days free. No credit card required. Plans from $39/month.

The secondary action opens or jumps to the demonstration, without a lead form. Keep its label accurate to the available asset. If only a static walkthrough is ready, do not label it a video.

The hero image should show the connection between a customer-facing quote and the resulting appointment. Use a readable desktop schedule with one customer phone view, or a simple three-step product sequence. Replace the current calculator-confidence overlay with an actual workflow state. Use fictional demo data visibly identified as an example.

Place a brief capability line below the offer: **Quotes and online booking · Recurring schedules · Crew tools · Invoices and payments**. This prevents the booking-led headline from making CleanQuote appear to be only a website widget.

The offer wording follows current public copy and local pricing constants. Recheck the live signup and checkout terms before publishing.

### B. Show the full booking flow

Proposed heading: **See what happens after someone visits your website.**

Use a 45–60 second recording or four accessible, captioned frames:

1. A homeowner enters service and property details in the embedded form.
2. The homeowner receives a generated quote by email. Text delivery is shown only with appropriate consent and an available messaging configuration.
3. They review and approve the quote, with a deposit step when required.
4. They choose an available appointment. Show the resulting visit in the business schedule.

Caption: **Your customer can move from quote to appointment online, using the options and availability your business provides.**

Show the actual steps. The widget does not currently display a live price on the form, so do not invent an instant on-page price reveal. Avoid claiming every inquiry books automatically or that every business is ready to accept bookings immediately after signup. Configuration, availability, service fit, and deposit/payment setup still matter.

Finish with Start Free Trial. The demonstration should explain the product before asking for more commitment.

### C. Feature overview based on the owner's work

Heading: **Keep the next step connected to the last one.**

Use six compact cards, each with a concrete benefit and an anchor to detail:

| Card | Proposed copy | Product evidence to show |
|---|---|---|
| Quote and book | Give customers a clear quote and a way to book an available appointment. | Branded quote and booking screen. |
| Plan recurring work | Keep recurring visits and cleaner assignments together on your schedule. | Week view with a recurring example. |
| Equip the crew | Put the day's visits, job details, checklists, and timers on their phones. | Cleaner mobile view. |
| Keep customers informed | Manage reminders and give customers access to their visits and invoices. | Automation settings and customer portal. |
| Invoice and collect | Send invoices with a payment link and see what still needs attention. | Invoice and payment status. |
| Understand the work | Compare estimated and actual hours and review how the business is performing. | Cleaner-efficiency or operating report. |

Keep each card short. Use the larger sections below for screenshots and operational detail. Messaging capabilities and add-on requirements must be labeled wherever relevant.

### D. Two detailed operational stories

**A schedule that fits recurring cleaning work.**

Proposed copy: “See the week, assign cleaners, and manage recurring visits in one place. Review proposed schedule changes before applying them.”

Pair `schedule-week.webp` with a focused Organizer view. Demonstrate preferred days, cleaners, arrival windows, and the proposed changes. Keep detailed scheduling rules in expandable help or a feature page. Do not promise mathematically optimal routing or guaranteed travel savings.

**Give your cleaners the details for the job.**

Proposed copy: “Your team can see their assigned visits, check the job details, complete checklists, and track their time from their phones.”

Use `mobile-schedule.webp` and `mobile-timesheets.webp`. Explain the current mobile web/home-screen installation accurately. Do not use App Store or Google Play badges unless those native apps actually exist.

### E. Make the pricing feedback loop memorable

Heading: **Compare what you estimated with what the cleaning took.**

Proposed copy: “CleanQuote estimates labor time from the home and service details, then uses recorded visit hours in its calibration process. You can review estimated and actual time as your team completes work.”

Visual sequence: **Estimate → Record time → Review the difference → Calibrate future estimates**.

Show one clearly labeled example using the calculator, timer, and report. Do not fabricate a before-and-after performance result. Explain the relationship in plain language before presenting thresholds, percentages, and calibration settings.

The precise policy for automatic reductions needs reconciliation before publication. Do not reuse the current blanket approval promise. This discrepancy is documented in section 6.

### F. Payments, communications, and the rest of the office

Give invoices and payment collection a substantial screen-led section. Follow with a compact overview of customer records, portal access, automations, reporting, multiple locations, expenses, and imports.

Suggested payment heading: **Keep the invoice connected to the work.**

Suggested copy: “Manage invoices and payment status alongside your customers and visits, with online payment links when your payment account is connected.”

Customer prepaid balances appear in the recent product source. Treat them as a supporting feature pending a current product demonstration, not a headline promise or a claim of automatic financial correctness.

Give the Communications Hub its own labeled card: **Optional Communications Hub**. Explain dedicated-number texting and calling, price, activation requirements, and any applicable usage charges. Do not present them as part of the base $39 offer.

AI connections can be a later, optional feature detail once the supported customer setup is demonstrated. Connecting an external assistant is different from shipping a ready-to-use AI receptionist. Do not use an autonomous voice-agent promise in the hero.

### G. Credibility and switching

Move the founder story closer to the product evidence. Use a real photograph, Brandon's name and role, and a concise explanation of one problem that prompted the product. Confirm the exact founder wording before publishing first-person biographical claims.

Preferred proof order:

1. A verifiable product demonstration.
2. A disclosed founder-operator example with actual evidence.
3. Permissioned customer testimony with a name, business, and specific result.
4. Measured outcomes once there is enough reliable data to substantiate them.

If customer testimonials are unavailable, omit that module. Do not substitute fictitious reviews, decorative star ratings, or customer counts. A founder demonstration is enough to start.

Switching heading: **Bring your customers with you.**

Explain supported CSV imports, the Jobber preset, and what needs to be checked after import. Show the mapping or preview step. List supported record types only after confirming the current importer. State how help is obtained only if an actual support channel and service exist.

Do not promise free concierge migration, complete payment-method migration, or an afternoon-long migration unless CleanQuote is prepared to deliver those specific promises.

## 5. Visual and interaction direction

- Retain CleanQuote's recognizable navy, orange, and light background. Use one consistent filled treatment for the primary trial action. Do not copy competitors' branding or assets.
- Reduce long explanatory paragraphs. Give each major feature one owner problem, a short explanation, and a readable product screen.
- Use existing screenshots as starting assets, then recapture screens that no longer match the product. Prefer focused crops over full dashboards with unreadable labels.
- Show residential-cleaning details: recurring frequency, cleaner assignment, arrival windows, home details, and customer instructions. Avoid generic technology illustrations.
- Put trial access before media on narrow screens. Keep a usable mobile navigation and persistent access to Start Free Trial without covering content.
- Keep core features visible without a carousel or hover interaction. If desktop tabs are used, provide an accessible stacked mobile equivalent.
- Use a poster image and click-to-play video. Include captions and a text walkthrough. Do not force audio or load a large hero video before the visitor asks to watch.
- Ensure keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, descriptive screenshot text, and a usable zoom view. These are implementation acceptance criteria, not findings from a completed accessibility audit.
- Avoid entrance popups and overlapping chat/survey widgets in the initial version. The first screen should make the product and trial action easy to understand.

## 6. Accuracy and conversion issues to fix before publishing

| Finding | Evidence and implication | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| Calculator dominates the opening | The current hero, first facts strip, and early detailed sections emphasize calculation mechanics. | Show the connected workflow and product breadth first, then explain calibration. |
| Mobile header prioritizes existing users | `Nav.astro` hides the trial link below the small breakpoint while leaving Log In visible. | Keep the trial action accessible on mobile. Verify in an actual narrow viewport during implementation. |
| Trial and login use the same destination | `site.ts` and the README point both to the app root. This does not establish what a new visitor sees there. | Test the signed-out journey and link trial buttons to a verified signup entry point. Do not invent a route. Preserve campaign attribution through signup. |
| Hourly rate is described as margin | Current local homepage copy equates the selected rate with margin. Rate is a selling input, not profit after costs. | Use “Estimated labor hours × your chosen hourly rate.” Remove the margin equivalence. |
| Calibration approval claim conflicts with recent code | Homepage FAQ says prices never decrease without approval. `decideZone` has an `extreme_outlier` branch with automatic downward adjustment, and the calibration runner consumes the analysis. | Reconcile the intended policy with the deployed implementation. Until then, use narrower descriptive copy. This review did not change or execute calibration. |
| Messaging statements can overpromise | A phone number alone is not consent. Recent widget copy documents an unchecked text-consent box. | Describe email delivery, with text delivery only when supported and consented. Explain Hub requirements separately. |
| Report count appears stale | Homepage says ten reports. The recent reports page includes the expanded report suite as well as existing report cards. | Name valuable reports instead of hardcoding a count. Confirm the current live catalog if a count is desired. |
| “Same features” obscures differences | Homepage itself lists differing automation allowances and team capabilities. | Explain shared core tools and actual limits explicitly. |
| “Most Popular” lacks evidence in this review | The featured plan has that badge, but subscription data was not examined. | Substantiate it or replace it with an audience-based label. |
| Setup and outcome promises are too absolute | Existing copy promises afternoon setup and uses universal or numerical descriptions of wasted time. | Use conditional or demonstrable examples. Do not imply observed typical results without evidence. |

The attempted browser viewport override did not produce a trustworthy narrow-screen capture. The mobile header finding above is source-based, not a completed live mobile test. No signup, message sending, booking, payment, or production settings were changed in this research.

## 8. How to determine whether the update works

Measure **completed new trials**, not just button clicks. Attribute trials to eligible marketing-site visitors and keep the definition stable between versions. Track desktop and mobile, traffic source, and new versus returning visitors separately where feasible.

Recommended event sequence:

| Event | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Homepage viewed | Eligible marketing visit, excluding internal/test activity. |
| Trial CTA clicked | Include placement: header, hero, feature, pricing, or footer. |
| Signup started | Visitor reaches and begins the verified registration flow. |
| Trial created | A real new trial account is successfully created, recorded once. |
| First draft quote saved | Early activation signal without requiring a customer message. |
| Later paid conversion | Quality check once the trial cohort has had time to mature. |

Track demonstration starts/completions, feature-detail engagement, and pricing views as diagnostic signals. They are not substitutes for trial and activation outcomes. Do not collect customer details, quote content, or payment information in marketing events. Review the existing privacy disclosure before adding a new analytics provider.

Primary experiment:

- Version A: the proposed website-to-booking headline and demonstration.
- Version B: an operations-led opening, for example “Run your cleaning business from the first quote to the next visit.”
- Keep the offer and the rest of the page consistent for an interpretable hero test.
- Success: more completed trials per eligible visitor without a material decline in first-draft-quote activation or later paid conversion.

Establish the actual baseline before selecting sample size or duration. No traffic volume or present conversion rate was supplied. If traffic is too low for a useful experiment, use a sequential launch comparison plus short owner usability sessions, and acknowledge that traffic changes can confound the result. Do not promise a lift or declare a winner from a few signups.

Release acceptance: all proposed claims verified, all CTAs reach the intended destination, mobile trial access works, media and screenshots are readable, keyboard use works, trial-created events are deduplicated, attribution survives the site-to-app transition, and page speed has not materially regressed.
